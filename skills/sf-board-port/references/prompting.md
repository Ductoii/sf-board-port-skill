# Seedance prompting and cinematic pacing

## Video format — preserve this shape

Every video prompt is one paragraph on one line. The following illustrates syntax; substitute the actual cast/location, never leave placeholders in a deliverable.

```text
Scene 001: REF BG1a, CHAR1a, CHAR2a, AL1a: /filmlook: /softbox: photorealistic 35mm film quality, ARRI ALEXA aesthetic, authentic Kodak color science, visible 35mm film grain, soft highlight halation, transform all referenced subjects into real live-action humans with raw unretouched matte skin micro-texture, visible skin pores, fine peach fuzz, subtle natural skin imperfections and physical fabric weave, warm window light, 30fps, widescreen 16:9 frame, clear dialogue up front, no background music score, only soft natural ambience, every spoken line is in natural English, each referenced character appears once only in the live scene, every background extra is a different stranger in muted clothes, reference-sheet panels and captions never appear in the live-action frame, BG1a = the one location for every live-action shot: a small records office with one wooden desk between two chairs and a window at rear, CHAR1a = Maya, a brown-skinned woman of 30 in a blue cotton shirt and grey trousers, hair in a low bun, neutral expression, realistic unretouched matte skin with visible pores, physical fabric weave, must match exactly, CHAR2a = Elias, a fair-skinned man of 45 in a brown wool jacket and cream shirt, short dark hair and clean-shaven face, realistic unretouched matte skin with visible pores, physical fabric weave, must match exactly, AL1a = one dark navy leather binder with a brass clasp | shot 1: /midpoint: static 40mm medium shot, CHAR1a standing left holding closed AL1a in both hands; CHAR2a standing right with empty hands, lips closed | shot 2: /closecrop: static three-quarter 75mm close-up on the single CHAR1a, holding closed AL1a, VO: "Here is the record." lips in sync, others keep lips still | shot 3: /dropdetail: close shot of CHAR1a offering AL1a; CHAR2a grips it with both hands, CHAR1a releases completely and withdraws empty hands | shot 4: /midpoint: static 40mm medium shot, CHAR1a standing left, empty hands; CHAR2a standing right holding closed AL1a in both hands, lips closed, hold
```

Retain the nominated projects' exact header, tag spelling, separators and voice convention. Change only content/definitions needed for this story. Lighting tone tags vary with the scene. English is this workflow's default, not permission to translate an existing non-English script without instruction.

- First and last shots: explicit present positions/postures/props, quiet closed lips; last `lips closed, hold`. No technical second counts. These handles are intentional editing room; do not remove them to claim faster pacing.
- Middle shots: a close shot of the one speaker per turn, `VO: "..." lips in sync, others keep lips still`. Separate consequential hand actions into readable inserts; do not hide a six-step prop exchange behind a reaction close-up.
- An over-the-shoulder shot keeps the foreground listener's face hidden and respects the established dialogue axis. Reaction shots show a different narrative response, not interchangeable filler.
- Ground visible writing on the actual paper/phone/sign. Phone screens face the person; use an over-the-shoulder insert for readable screen information. Archival evidence plays only within the physical display, without spawning its people in the live room.
- The header must agree with state changes. A character wearing WITHOUT an apron after removal cannot also have a header definition locking the apron on. A glass filled during the scene is not perpetually described as empty.

## Pacing and camera

Reference 83/Millionaire-style projects for the **prompt language and editorial rhythm**, not a fixed target clip count. Preserve the important story turns and causal links; aim for a faithful usable film rather than exhaustive prop choreography. Different dialogue volumes legitimately yield different clip counts. Generated clip count × dur is available source material, not the final edit's runtime.

Compose the beat before choosing a move:

- Establish geography at scene changes. Use a meaningful mix of medium, three-quarter, over-the-shoulder, low angle, off-centre negative space, tight reaction, and hand/evidence inserts. 32–40mm suits space; 50–85mm suits dialogue/detail.
- Static coverage is dominant. A rough 80/20 static/moving preference is guidance, not a quota; do not add arbitrary camera motion to satisfy it. Track/follow when an actor actually crosses space; a restrained push-in serves a turn or decision, at most one per clip. A gentle pan/tilt is justified when it reveals new information and stays in the existing tag syntax.
- Distinguish editing handles from dramatic pauses. Avoid multiplying clips for tiny gestures; split when speaking load or meaningful actions exceed what one source clip can perform.
- Audit actual camera words in the shot bodies. If most are static and only a handful move, report that honestly; naming ARRI/35mm/film grain does not establish cinematic direction or finished quality.

Local templates avoid: `everyone holding still`, `the whole room frozen`, `no plastic`, `black face patches`, `subsurface scattering`, `3D wireframe`, `skin sheen`, `jib-arm crane`, `30-degree orbital arc`. Use concrete positive descriptions of real skin/fabric and simple camera placement. These are workflow conventions, not proven universal claims about a model's internals.

## CHAR reference — one step, four views

Use a single photorealistic landscape 16:9 white-studio sheet with FOUR vertical panels in one row: FACE CLOSE-UP, FRONT, BACK, SIDE. The sharp front head-and-shoulders close-up takes 30 percent; the remaining three panels divide 70 percent equally and show full bodies from head to soles, aligned feet and clear margins. Thin grey dividers; the four exact labels in the footer. Same face/body/outfit/accessories across panels, neutral relaxed posture/expression, arms naturally at sides, empty hands, even studio light, real skin pores and fabric weave. Append the individual face anatomy, hair, age, skin tone and outfit. Use original fictional faces, no celebrity imitation.

Costume variants attach the base CHAR directly; no separate vertical BASE portrait stage. Keep transient handheld props off sheets. Hats/jewellery may remain when habitual; removed/wet/carried variants are ordinarily handled in video text.

## BG reference — four views of one empty place

One landscape 16:9 reference sheet in a 2×2 grid of four equal panels, thin white dividers. Main view / left view / right view / useful spatial detail, all of the same architecture, furniture, light and weather. No people, portraits, captions or panel numbers. Real believable materials and 35mm photography. Different persistent day/night conditions may use linked variants. Do not put the entire story cast or stage a dramatic action in the BG sheet.
